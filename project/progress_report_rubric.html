Project progress report rubric (50 points)

The progress report is one page, due through the Canvas assignment by 11:59 PM on Friday, March 26, and is 5% of the course grade. It is a group assignment: all members of a team receive the team grade. Use progress_report_template.md, whose sections are the criteria below in order.

Each criterion is worth 10 points and is scored in one of four bands.

Criterion Excellent (9-10) Good (7-8) Fair (5-6) Weak (0-4)
What is done
Data, preprocessing, baseline implementation		 Data access and preprocessing are finished and described concretely enough to repeat, and the baseline runs end to end. Most of the pipeline is working; one piece is described without evidence that it runs. Work has started but the baseline does not yet run, and the report does not say what is blocking it. No evidence that anything runs.
Baseline results
One table or figure on the evaluation split		 A result on the team's own evaluation split, with the split stated, enough to tell whether the project is viable. A result is reported; the split or the sample size is unstated. A number appears with no indication of what it was computed on. No results.
Changes from the proposal
What moved, and why		 Changes are stated plainly with the reason, including anything that turned out not to work. Changes stated; the reasoning is thin. Changes mentioned in passing, or the report reads as though the proposal never changed when it clearly did. Contradicts the proposal without acknowledging it.
Remaining plan
Tasks by week, with owners		 Tasks mapped to the weeks left before the presentation, each with an owner, and the total work fits the time. A sequenced plan; owners or dates are partly missing. A list of intentions with no sequence or owners. No plan for the remaining weeks.
Honest assessment
What is not working, and whether the plan fits		 Says what is behind or not working, and reaches a defensible judgement about whether the original scope still fits or the fallback should start now. Acknowledges difficulties; stops short of judging whether the scope still fits. Reports only good news in a project that evidently has problems. Claims progress the results do not show.

The report is graded on the quality of the evidence and the judgement, not on how far along the team is. A team that reports a baseline that did not work, explains why, and switches to its fallback can score full marks. A team that reports smooth progress it cannot show cannot.

This is also the last point at which a scope problem is cheap to fix. If the plan no longer fits the weeks that remain, say so here rather than in April.
