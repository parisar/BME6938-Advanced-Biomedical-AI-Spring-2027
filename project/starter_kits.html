Project starter kits

Starting points for the suggested problems, for teams that do not arrive with their own topic. Each kit gives a question, data and how to get it, a baseline to beat, a plan, how to evaluate, and pitfalls. Treat them as a starting point: a good proposal narrows the question, names a hypothesis, and says what result would change your mind.

Before you pick: data access

  • Open data (download now): MedMNIST, the Kather colorectal H&E datasets, PTB-XL, MedQA, PubMedQA, the MIMIC-IV Clinical Database Demo (100 patients), and Synthea synthetic patients.
  • Credentialed or registration data (start in Week 1): MIMIC-IV, MIMIC-IV-Note, MIMIC-CXR, and eICU on PhysioNet need CITI human-subjects training plus a PhysioNet credentialing request, which together can take several weeks. CheXpert needs a free registration with Stanford.
  • Rules for credentialed data: work on HiPerGator, never on Colab or a personal laptop you share; never paste or upload records to online AI services (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, or any hosted API); never commit data to GitHub. Use local open models for anything that touches patient text.
  • Compute: every kit below is sized for one GPU on HiPerGator, and most can be prototyped on a CPU.

1. Long clinical notes with a small open LLM (Week 4)

Question. For questions about a long discharge summary, does a long-context small model beat a short-context model plus retrieval? Data. MIMIC-IV-Note discharge summaries (credentialed). Build questions whose answers are in the structured MIMIC-IV tables (discharge medications, primary diagnosis codes, length of stay), so you get ground truth without manual labels. Baseline. A small open model (about 1B to 8B parameters) reading only the first part of the note that fits its window. Plan. Compare (a) the full note in a long context, (b) chunk, embed, and retrieve the top passages into a short context, (c) the baseline. Vary note length. Evaluate. Exact-match or F1 against the structured answers, by note length; also memory and time per note. Pitfalls. Notes and structured tables can disagree; check a sample by hand. Run models locally on HiPerGator.

2. Reasoning versus non-reasoning on medical question answering (Week 5)

Question. When does "thinking" help a small model on medical questions, and is it worth the tokens? Optionally, does a short LoRA fine-tune beat prompting? Data. MedQA (USMLE-style) and PubMedQA (open). Baseline. The same small open model answering directly, with no reasoning prompt. Plan. A reasoning prompt versus a direct answer; then a LoRA fine-tune on a training split; compare accuracy and tokens per question. Evaluate. Accuracy with bootstrap confidence intervals; calibration of the model's stated confidence; tokens and time. Pitfalls. Benchmark contamination (the model may have seen the questions); report it as a limitation and, if possible, test on questions you rewrite.

3. Label efficiency of imaging foundation models (Week 6)

Question. As labeled data shrinks, how do a linear probe and LoRA fine-tuning of a pretrained encoder compare? Data. NCT-CRC-HE-100K or the smaller Kather 2016 tiles (colorectal H&E, CC BY 4.0), or a MedMNIST dataset. Baseline. The same architecture trained from scratch, and the encoder with random weights. Plan. Encoders: DINOv2 and a pathology encoder if you can get access (UNI is gated on Hugging Face). Label budgets of 1, 10, 100 per class and the full set. Evaluate. Balanced accuracy with confidence intervals across several random label samples. Pitfalls. Tiles from the same slide or patient must not appear in both training and test sets.

4. Self-supervised pretraining for ECG (Week 6)

Question. For ECG, does masked reconstruction or contrastive pretraining give better features with few labels? Data. PTB-XL (PhysioNet, open access, CC BY 4.0): 10-second 12-lead ECGs at 500 Hz, with recommended folds. Baseline. Supervised training from scratch; a random-weight encoder. Plan. Pretrain a small 1D encoder both ways on the training folds, then probe and fine-tune on diagnostic labels with few labels per class. Evaluate. Macro AUROC on the official test fold; label-efficiency curves. Pitfalls. The Week 6 notebook shows a tiny masked autoencoder can learn almost nothing; check reconstructions and the loss curve before trusting any probe result. Use the provided folds so patients do not leak.

5. Zero-shot versus fine-tuned vision-language models for chest X-ray (Week 7)

Question. How far is zero-shot classification from a few-label linear probe on chest X-ray findings, and do subgroups differ? Data. CheXpert (registration) or MIMIC-CXR (credentialed). Baseline. A linear probe on a general CLIP image encoder. Plan. BiomedCLIP or another medical vision-language encoder: zero-shot with several prompt wordings, then linear probes with small label budgets. Evaluate. AUROC per finding with confidence intervals; the same metrics by sex and age group. Pitfalls. Labels extracted from reports are noisy; uncertain labels need an explicit policy.

6. Patient timelines as tokens versus gradient boosting (Week 8)

Question. Does a small timeline transformer beat gradient-boosted trees for an ICU outcome, and which stays calibrated when time moves on? Data. MIMIC-IV (credentialed); its anchor year groups allow a train-on-earlier, test-on-later split. eICU for a second site. Baseline. Gradient-boosted trees on hand-built features from the first 24 hours. Plan. Tokenize timelines as in ETHOS (codes, quantile tokens, time-gap tokens); train a small decoder; predict in-ICU mortality. Evaluate. AUROC, AUPRC (low prevalence), and calibration, on a random split and on a later-years split. Pitfalls. Leakage from events after the prediction time; define the prediction time first and cut the timeline there.

7. Multimodal fusion when a modality is missing (Week 7)

Question. Does late or intermediate fusion of chest X-rays and EHR data still help when one modality is missing? Data. MIMIC-IV with MIMIC-CXR (credentialed), linked by patient and time. Baseline. The best single-modality model. Plan. Late fusion (average predictions) versus intermediate fusion (combine embeddings), trained with random modality dropout; test with each modality removed. Evaluate. AUROC with confidence intervals for complete and missing-modality test sets. Pitfalls. Patients with imaging differ from patients without it; missingness itself is a signal.

8. Retrieval for clinical question answering (Week 9)

Question. Does retrieval improve accuracy and faithfulness of a small model's answers, and when should it abstain? Data. PubMedQA (questions with source abstracts) and MedQA; a corpus of PubMed abstracts. Baseline. The same model without retrieval. Plan. Embed and retrieve passages; vary top-k and an abstention threshold, as in Almanac. Evaluate. Accuracy; faithfulness (is each claim supported by a retrieved passage, judged on a sample by hand); abstention rate on answerable questions. Pitfalls. An abstaining system looks safe on adversarial tests; always report how often it abstains on real questions.

9. A tool-using agent for a structured clinical task (Week 9)

Question. Where do tool-using agents fail on a structured clinical task, such as medication reconciliation or a lab-trend question? Data. Synthea synthetic patients or the MIMIC-IV Clinical Database Demo (open); no credentialed data needed. Baseline. The same model answering from a pasted record without tools. Plan. Build two or three read-only tools (get labs, get medications, get notes); run a ReAct-style loop on 50 to 100 tasks with known answers. Evaluate. Task success; a failure taxonomy (wrong tool, wrong arguments, misread result, reasoning error). Pitfalls. Keep every tool read-only; log full trajectories so failures can be classified.

10. Shortcut audit of a medical imaging classifier (Week 12)

Question. Can the hospital or scanner be predicted from an imaging model's features, and does that hurt performance at a new site? Data. Two chest X-ray sources (for example CheXpert and MIMIC-CXR), or two MedMNIST-style sources. Baseline. Internal test performance. Plan. Train on one source; probe the features for source; test on the other source; try a mitigation (for example balancing or source-adversarial training). Evaluate. Source-prediction accuracy from features; internal versus external AUROC with confidence intervals. Pitfalls. Differences in labels between sources can look like a shortcut; compare findings both sources label the same way.

11. Uncertainty under distribution shift (Week 12)

Question. Do conformal prediction sets keep their promised coverage when the test data shift? Data. Any classifier above, with an in-distribution and a shifted test set (another site, later years, or another stain). Baseline. Coverage and set size in distribution. Plan. Split conformal prediction at 90% target coverage; measure coverage under shift; try a simple fix such as recalibrating on a small sample from the new setting. Evaluate. Coverage, average set size, and coverage by subgroup. Pitfalls. Coverage is a promise about averages; report subgroups, where it often fails first.

12. Reproduce a key result and extend it (any week)

Question. Does a central result of one course paper hold, and does it survive one change you choose (a new dataset, subgroup, or metric)? Plan. Pick a paper with public code and data; reproduce one table or figure; then make one extension. Evaluate. How close your reproduction is, with confidence intervals; what the extension changes. Pitfalls. Budget most of your time for the reproduction; contact the authors early if something does not match.
