# Project proposal rubric (50 points)

The proposal is one page, due through the Canvas assignment by 11:59 PM on Friday, February 19, and is 5% of the
course grade. It is a group assignment: all members of a team receive the team grade. Use
[proposal_template.md](proposal_template.md), whose sections are the criteria below in order.

Each criterion is worth 10 points and is scored in one of four bands.

| Criterion | Excellent (9-10) | Good (7-8) | Fair (5-6) | Weak (0-4) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Open problem and contribution**<br>The problem, why it matters, what is new | A reader outside the team understands the problem, its clinical or technical stakes, and what this work adds that prior work does not. | Problem and contribution are clear; the relation to prior work is thin. | The problem is stated, but the contribution is unclear or is really a restatement of prior work. | The reader cannot tell what problem the team intends to solve. |
| **Data and access**<br>Dataset, scale, access route, prediction target | Named dataset with its size and licence, an access route the team can actually complete before March, and a prediction target defined precisely enough to compute. | Dataset and target are clear; scale or access is stated loosely. | A dataset is named without size, access route, or a well-defined target. | No dataset, or one the team has no realistic route to. |
| **Methods and baseline**<br>Model, adaptation, and what it is compared against | Pretrained model and adaptation method are named and suited to the data, with a baseline strong enough that beating it would mean something. | Reasonable method and a named baseline; the choice of either is lightly justified. | Method is vague, or the baseline is too weak to support the intended claim. | No baseline, or methods that do not match the stated problem. |
| **Hypothesis and evaluation plan**<br>Testable statements, metrics, splits | One or two statements that could come out false, with metrics, splits, and at least one analysis beyond a single aggregate number, such as calibration, subgroups, or a shift test. | Testable hypothesis with metrics and splits; the analysis stops at one aggregate metric. | Hypothesis is vague or unfalsifiable, or the evaluation plan is only a metric name. | Neither a hypothesis nor a plan that could test one. |
| **Risks and fallback**<br>What could block this, and the version that still finishes | Names the specific thing most likely to block the project, with a reduced version that is still worth doing and can be finished in the time. | A risk and a fallback, one of them generic. | Risks listed without a fallback, or a fallback that is really the same project. | Neither, or a plan with no slack in a ten-week term. |

A proposal is graded on whether the project is well posed and finishable, not on whether the idea is ambitious. A
modest question with a clear baseline and an honest evaluation plan scores higher than a sweeping one that cannot be
carried out by April.

Projects use open-access data by default. A student who already holds their own PhysioNet credentials may use them in
their own storage, but no team should plan around credentialing that has not already been granted: it takes weeks and
is not guaranteed to arrive before the work starts.
