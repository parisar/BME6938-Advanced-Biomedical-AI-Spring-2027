# Paper presentation and critique rubrics

Each Thursday the class holds two paper sessions, one per period. Each session has a **presentation group** (20 minutes), a **critique group** (15 minutes), and class discussion (15 minutes). The two groups are graded independently. Who grades and how individual grades are set is described in [grading.md](grading.md).

General expectations for both groups:

- Ground every claim in the paper. Use technical language accurately.
- Every member presents, with content and speaking time shared equally.
- Grading emphasizes clarity, rigor, and depth of understanding, not polish.

## Presentation group (100 points, 20 minutes)

Each criterion is worth 20 points and is scored in one of four bands.

| Criterion | Excellent (18-20) | Good (14-17) | Fair (10-13) | Weak (0-9) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Overview and motivation**<br>The problem, why it matters, and the main contribution | A newcomer understands the problem, its clinical or technical stakes, and the contribution within the first few minutes. | Problem and contribution are clear; motivation is thin or generic. | Problem is stated, but the contribution is unclear or confused with prior work. | The audience cannot tell what problem the paper solves. |
| **Methods and technical approach**<br>Data, models, and key design choices | Data (source, scale, splits), model, and key design choices are explained correctly and at the right depth, including why each choice was made. | Correct and complete, with little on why choices were made. | Superficial or partly incorrect; key components are missing. | Substantially incorrect or missing. |
| **Results and evidence**<br>Main results, metrics, and baselines, interpreted | Main results are interpreted against baselines and metrics, including what they do and do not show. | Main results and baselines are presented with some interpretation. | Results are shown with little interpretation, or baselines are missing. | Results are missing, misreported, or unexplained. |
| **Strengths and contributions**<br>What the paper does well, relative to prior work | Concrete, evidence-based strengths, placed relative to prior work. | Concrete strengths with limited comparison to prior work. | Generic strengths ("novel", "well written"). | No strengths identified, or praise without support. |
| **Presentation quality**<br>Structure, slides, delivery, and timing | Logical flow, clear visuals in the group's own words, on time, all members contribute equally. | Clear overall; minor timing or slide issues. | Hard to follow, text-heavy slides, timing clearly off, or uneven speaking time. | Disorganized, slides read aloud, or major timing problems. |

Slides should support understanding rather than replicate the paper. Interpret results; do not only show them.

## Critique group (100 points, 15 minutes)

Each criterion is worth 20 points and is scored in one of four bands.

| Criterion | Excellent (18-20) | Good (14-17) | Fair (10-13) | Weak (0-9) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Summary of core claims**<br>Accurate restatement before critiquing | Claims are restated accurately and concisely, separating the main claim from secondary ones. | Accurate, but misses some nuance. | Partly inaccurate, or too long for the time available. | Misrepresents the paper. |
| **Methodological critique**<br>Data, design, validation, and missing controls | Specific, technical issues tied to sections, figures, or tables of the paper, prioritized by importance. | Valid and specific points, but not prioritized or partly superficial. | Mostly generic points ("the dataset is small"). | Absent or incorrect. |
| **Results and interpretation critique**<br>Do the results support the claims? | Clearly separates what is shown from what is claimed and tests whether the evidence supports each claim. | Identifies some gaps between evidence and claims. | Restates the results with little evaluation. | Absent. |
| **Limitations, biases, and ethics**<br>Beyond what the authors state | Goes beyond the authors' own list with plausible, specific risks (generalization, bias, clinical, ethical). | Sound, but mostly repeats the authors' list. | Generic concerns not tied to this paper. | Absent. |
| **Constructive suggestions**<br>Concrete, realistic improvements | Concrete, feasible experiments or analyses that would address the main critiques. | Reasonable suggestions, but vague. | Generic ("use more data"). | None, or purely negative. |

Critique the paper, not the presentation group. Show you understand the paper before critiquing it, and end constructively.
