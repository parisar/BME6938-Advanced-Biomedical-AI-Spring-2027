# **Paper Presentation & Critique Rubric**

Each Thursday the class holds two paper sessions, one per period, in which students examine current research related to the topic of the week.

Each paper session includes:

- **One Presentation Group** (summarizes and explains the paper) (20 min)

- **One Critique Group** (analyzes, evaluates, and challenges the paper) (15 min)

- **Discussion** (15 min)

## General notes:

- All claims should be grounded in the paper's content.

- Technical language should be used accurately.

- Groups are expected to coordinate internally, with balanced participation.

- Grading emphasizes **clarity, rigor, and depth of understanding**, not presentation polish alone.

- All group members are expected to present, with content and speaking time distributed equally among them.

- Both groups are graded **independently**.

## **Paper Presentation Group (100 Points Total)** 

### **1. Paper Overview & Motivation - 20 Points**

Clearly explains:

- The problem being addressed

- Why it matters (scientific, clinical, or technical motivation)

- The paper's main contribution(s)

**Expectations:  
** The audience unfamiliar with the paper should understand *what problem the authors are solving and why it is important*.

### **2. Methodology & Technical Approach - 20 Points**

Accurately describes:

- Data used (type, scale, source, assumptions)

- Model(s), algorithms, or system architecture

- Key design choices and workflows

**Expectations:  
** *Explanation should be technically correct, appropriately detailed for the audience, and avoid superficial summaries*.

### **3. Results & Evidence - 20 Points** 

- Main quantitative and/or qualitative results

- Evaluation metrics and baselines

- Key figures or tables from the paper

**Expectations:**  
Results should be interpreted, not just shown. Students should explain *what the results demonstrate*.

### **4. Strengths & Contributions - 20 Points** 

Identifies:

- What the paper does well

- Novelty or significance relative to prior work

- Practical or theoretical impact

**Expectations:  
** *Strengths should be concrete and evidence-based, not generic praise.*

### **5. Presentation Quality & Organization - 20 Points** 

Assesses:

- Logical structure and pacing

- Slide clarity and visual quality

- Professional delivery and time management

**Expectations:  
** *Slides should support understanding rather than replicate the paper verbatim.*

## **Paper Critique Group (100 Points Total)**

##  

### **1. Summary of Core Claims - 20 Points** 

Briefly restates:

- The central research question or hypothesis the paper is attempting to address

- The paper's main claims and conclusions

**Expectations:**  
*Demonstrates accurate understanding before critiquing.*

### **2. Methodological Critique - 20 Points** 

Critically evaluates:

- Appropriateness of data, models, and assumptions

- Experimental design and validation strategy

- Missing controls, comparisons, or analyses

**Expectations:  
** *Critique should be specific, technical, and evidence-based, not opinion-driven.*

### **3. Results & Interpretation Critique - 20 Points** 

Analyzes:

- Whether the results support the claims

- Limitations of metrics, baselines, or analyses

- Overstated or unsupported conclusions

**Expectations:  
** Students should distinguish *what is shown* from *what is claimed*.

### **4. Limitations, Biases & Ethical Considerations - 20 Points**

Discusses:

- Known or unaddressed limitations

- Generalization risks or failure modes

- Ethical, clinical, or societal concerns (if applicable)

**Expectations:**  
*Should go beyond what the authors explicitly state when appropriate.*

### **5. Constructive Suggestions & Future Work - 20 Points**

Proposes:

- Concrete improvements to methods or evaluation

- Follow-up experiments or extensions

- Alternative approaches

**Expectations:  
** Feedback should be *constructive and realistic*, not merely critical.
