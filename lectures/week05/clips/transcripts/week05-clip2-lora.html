LoRA: fine-tuning with a small fraction of the weights

Week 5, clip 2 of 4. This is the narration of the clip, which an AI-generated voice reads in the narrated version; the silent version shows the same steps with on-screen captions only.

Full fine-tuning with the Adam optimizer stores about 16 bytes for every parameter: the weight, its gradient, and 2 optimizer states. That is about ten gigabytes for a small model, and over a thousand for a 70B model.

Look at one weight matrix in the small Qwen3 model: a query projection with 2,048 rows and 1,024 columns, about 2 million weights. With LoRA, it stays frozen.

LoRA adds two thin matrices beside it. A takes the token down to just eight numbers, and B brings it back up. The output is W times x, plus B times A times x.

B and A together have 24,576 weights. That is about 1.2% of the frozen matrix, and only these are trained.

B starts at zero, so at the first step B times A times x is zero, and the model gives exactly the original output. Training then moves only B and A.

Add LoRA to the query, key, value, and output projections in all 28 layers, and it trains 2,293,760 weights. That is about 0.4% of the model.

Because the base model never changes, one copy can serve many tasks. Each task, such as discharge summaries or radiology impressions, needs only its own small adapter, about 4.6 megabytes.
